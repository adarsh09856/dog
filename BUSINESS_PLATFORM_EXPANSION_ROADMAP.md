# Kodewaves business platform expansion roadmap

Status: planning, expanded 29 October 2026. This is an expanded product direction, not a claim that the listed capabilities exist. Keep the current client-bundle work in `codex/client-bundle-theme` and the voice work in its separate checkout. The user has stopped merging. Do not switch, reset, merge, or deploy either checkout until that instruction changes.

## Product direction

The [AI platform control plan](AI_PLATFORM_CONTROL_PLAN.md) defines the shared assistant, action permissions, approval and recovery path, complete module coverage, delivery order and acceptance checklist. All new business panels and integrations must use that action contract so the assistant can operate the delivered platform consistently.

Build a configurable business operating platform with voice AI, omnichannel communications, customer relationships, workflow automation and optional industry modules. Support the owner-operated SaaS and a separately packaged customer installation. Customers configure their workspace and connect their own accounts within the plan, module, usage and model capabilities granted by the owner. The owner alone sets the grant and can suspend/revoke it; tenant admins may configure or reduce allowed limits but cannot raise them. Design for multiple languages and accessibility, with capabilities determined by the connected model and provider.

Do not promise every industry, jurisdiction, social feature, device-linking method or model language until the specific journey is implemented and accepted. Rank opportunities with customer evidence, delivery cost, provider access and maintenance capacity. This catalog is broad rather than exhaustive.

## Shared platform capabilities

1. **Workspace and administration:** organizations, branches, teams, users, granular roles, invitations, activity logs, custom fields, localization, time zones, accessibility and configurable branding. Owner panel controls module availability, usage, license, support access and audit; client admins manage their permitted workspace.
2. **Customer and relationship data:** contacts, companies, identity matching and explicit merge review, consent and preferences, tags, ownership, custom objects, attachments, notes, activities and a chronological timeline. Every record and lookup is tenant-scoped.
3. **Unified conversations:** inbox for connected channels, assignment, queues, internal notes, SLA timers, status, search, handoff, attachments and AI summaries. Preserve original channel events and delivery status; never silently merge identities across organizations.
4. **Workflow automation:** visual trigger/condition/action builder with versioning, draft/test/publish, delays, schedules, loops with bounds, branching, human approvals, idempotency, retry/dead-letter queues, replay controls and an execution history. AI actions inherit user and tenant permissions and require confirmation for consequential operations.
5. **Knowledge and AI:** tenant-owned documents, retrieval permissions, citations where available, configurable agents and human escalation; voice and text reuse business tools without bypassing authorization. Measure speech-end to first audio, cold/warm behavior, interruptions and completion. Support provider-advertised languages without implying universal model coverage.
6. **Reporting and operations:** dashboards, funnel and service metrics, channel delivery, call quality, automation failures, export, scheduled reports, alerting, backup and restore. Define data freshness and metric semantics.
7. **Integration framework:** connection inventory, OAuth or provider-supported authentication, encrypted credentials, least privilege, webhook verification, secret rotation, rate limits, per-tenant isolation, idempotency, subscription renewal and reconnect flows. Provide API, signed webhooks and documented SDK/embed methods.
8. **Commercial control:** plan catalog, transparent pricing, module entitlements, resource and usage limits, trials, upgrades, expiry, payment reconciliation and invoices. The owner assigns maximum entitlements; workspace admins configure within them and cannot grant themselves more access. Enforce at API, worker and action boundaries in both SaaS and client editions.

## Authority, editions and client-install threat model

| Actor | May configure | Cannot grant or bypass |
|---|---|---|
| Kodewaves owner/superadmin | Product catalogue, plan and price, model/provider availability, maximum modules/limits, client licenses, installation/domain/IP policy, suspension/revocation, owner support access and platform-wide audit. | Must not access customer business data by default; exceptional support access is time-limited, purpose-bound, visible and audited. |
| Customer workspace admin | Users and roles within the granted role ceiling, workspace settings, business profile, enabled modules within plan, connected accounts, workflows, business data and local quotas up to owner limits. | Cannot change owner-issued plan, extend license, add modules above grant, disable integrity enforcement, change signing keys or access another tenant. |
| Customer member/operator | Data and actions granted by workspace roles and assigned branch/team scope. | Cannot manage owner billing/licensing or escalate own role; every write is re-authorized server-side. |
| AI assistant/automation | Only typed actions made available to the invoking user/workflow, tenant, plan, consent and state. | Has no standing superadmin role and cannot elevate permission by prompt, tool result, webhook payload or model output. |

**Client distribution threat model:** customer installation artifacts and manifests are signed; startup/runtime verification checks approved image/file digests and the license binds an installation identity to approved domains and optional IP policy. A detected mismatch should stop licensed application actions or enter the defined safe/unavailable mode, emit a signed owner status report and create an owner alert; connectivity loss follows a documented bounded grace policy. Verification, server-side entitlements and owner revocation reduce tampering risk. They cannot make a verifier impossible to replace when a hostile customer controls the host as root. The release claim must say which tamper classes are detected, how quickly, and what is enforced remotely; never promise cryptographic prevention of a root administrator patching their own server.

## Business types, workflows and operating rules

The business type is a starting configuration, not a hard-coded product fork. A workspace may select more than one type (for example, clinic + pharmacy, hotel + restaurant, manufacturer + distributor), turn on modules by plan, and rename customer-facing terms. Selecting a type must load a guided setup with recommended records, statuses, fields, roles, calendars, automations, reports and AI actions. Owners may publish templates; each customer can review and adapt them without editing application code.

### Business model families

| Family | Common operating loop | Shared records and rules |
|---|---|---|
| Appointment and scheduled service | Inquiry → qualify → select service/resource → find an available slot → confirm → remind → deliver → collect feedback/payment → reschedule or close. | Person/company, service, location, staff/resource, availability, booking, consent, payment and activity. Never confirm a slot until the calendar write succeeds; prevent overlapping capacity and honor lead time, buffers, holidays and cancellation policy. |
| Sales and negotiated work | Lead → qualify → assign → discovery → opportunity → quote/proposal → approval → win/loss → delivery handoff. | Lead, account, contact, pipeline, task, quote/version, product/service, owner and source. Require permission and a durable event for stage/owner changes; version quotes and retain approval history. |
| Order and transaction | Browse/request → validate stock/price → reserve → authorize payment → fulfill → deliver → support/return/refund. | Catalog, price list, cart/order, payment, inventory reservation, shipment, return and refund. The server validates price and stock; payment capture/refund is provider-verified and idempotent; never infer success from a browser redirect. |
| Case and service operations | Request → classify → prioritize → assign → respond → escalate → resolve → confirm → analyze. | Conversation, ticket/case, requester, queue, SLA clock, knowledge citation, attachment, resolution and satisfaction. Track waiting-on-customer vs waiting-on-staff separately; business hours and pause reasons must be explicit. |
| Inventory and production | Demand → plan → procure → receive/inspect → reserve/issue → produce/assemble → quality check → ship → reconcile. | SKU, unit, warehouse/bin, supplier, purchase order, lot/serial, stock movement, work order, quality result and shipment. Treat inventory as a ledger of immutable movements; use reservation/commit/release and concurrency checks instead of editable totals. |
| Project and professional delivery | Inquiry → scope → proposal → agreement → staffing → milestones/tasks → time/expense → review → invoice → close. | Client, engagement, scope/version, milestone, task, resource, timesheet, expense, deliverable, invoice and approval. Keep billable status, budget and write-off permissions explicit; prevent unapproved commitments. |
| Recurring membership/subscription | Enroll → consent → activate → renew/charge → deliver entitlement → pause/change/cancel → reconcile. | Customer, plan/version, subscription, entitlement, payment mandate, invoice and lifecycle event. Separate trial, grace, suspension and cancellation; a failed charge must not silently grant indefinite access or double-charge on retry. |
| Regulated or sensitive casework | Intake → identity/consent → collect minimum data → route to qualified staff → review/decision → notify → retain/delete by policy. | Case, subject, consent, restricted document, reviewer, decision, disclosure and audit. Apply least-privilege access, retention and human approval; AI may assist but must not make a diagnosis, eligibility, credit or legal decision unless an explicitly approved policy permits it. |

### App panel and module map

Every navigation item must correspond to a working, permission-checked journey. Shared modules may be reused across verticals, but each profile must configure labels, fields, state rules and visibility. ### Required logic contract for every feature

Before implementation, write each feature as a behavior contract with: actor and role, owning workspace/branch, source of truth, input schema, validation, permitted state transitions, concurrency/idempotency strategy, side effects, event/audit record, notification, failure/retry/compensation path, privacy/retention rule, accessibility states and acceptance scenario. A UI control without this contract is not a finished feature.

For each profile, publish a feature matrix with the following executable scenarios:

1. **Happy path:** a real user completes the main journey from setup to persisted result and downstream timeline/report update.
2. **Permission path:** the same request from a lower role, another branch and another tenant is rejected without leaking object existence or sensitive data.
3. **Duplicate/concurrent path:** webhook replay, double click, retry after timeout and two operators racing for capacity/stock produce one valid business outcome.
4. **Failure path:** provider timeout, database conflict, expired credentials, partial batch and worker restart show actionable status and safe retry/reconciliation.
5. **Change/cancel path:** edits preserve old quote/terms, emit audit events, release reservations when appropriate and notify the correct recipients only once.
6. **Language/accessibility path:** localized labels and dates, keyboard-only operation, screen-reader announcements, mobile layout and reduced motion remain usable.
7. **AI path:** the agent reads current authorized facts, proposes a typed action, requests confirmation at the configured risk level, rechecks preconditions and reports the durable result without repeating or fabricating success.

This contract applies equally to API, browser UI, background workers, import/export, voice/chat tools and client-install edition. It prevents a visually complete panel from being mistaken for a complete business process.

## Shared data, state machines and business logic

Every business pack must declare its canonical objects and event names over shared platform primitives. Do not create a separate, incompatible customer or conversation store for each vertical.

| Shared object | Required behavior and invariants |
|---|---|
| Workspace, branch, team, user and role | Every record has a workspace boundary; optional branch/team restrictions are enforced server-side in APIs, background jobs, exports, search and AI actions. Permissions are explicit per action and field class. |
| Person, organization and identity link | Keep source identifiers and provenance; deduplicate with proposed matches and human-reviewed merge; preserve consent, contact preference, legal basis and merge history. Never merge across workspaces. |
| Product/service/resource | Version name, terms, price, tax configuration, capacity and availability; a quote/order/booking keeps the version it used rather than changing retroactively. |
| Conversation, event and activity | Store channel/source, tenant, actor, timestamps, delivery state, correlation/idempotency key and redacted payload as needed; preserve original events and append corrections rather than silently rewriting history. |
| Task, case, booking, order, payment and stock movement | Use explicit status transitions, actor/time/reason, required preconditions and idempotency; enforce state transitions on the server and test stale/conflicting updates. |
| Document and consent | Track owner, purpose, sensitivity, consent/revocation, retention and access audit; signed URLs and exports must preserve tenant and expiry controls. |

Standard state machines must be configurable by business type while retaining required terminal and safety states:

- **Lead/opportunity:** `new → assigned → working → qualified → proposal → won/lost`; transitions require an actor, timestamp and reason; reopen/merge is audited; automation cannot mark `won` without the required order/agreement event.
- **Conversation/ticket:** `open → queued → assigned → waiting_on_customer/waiting_on_staff → resolved → closed`; SLA clocks follow configured business hours and pause causes; a new inbound event may reopen by policy.
- **Appointment/job:** `requested → held → confirmed → checked_in/in_progress → completed`; alternatives include `cancelled`, `no_show`, `reschedule_pending`; hold has expiry; confirm requires successful capacity reservation, and completion requires result/evidence where the workflow demands it.
- **Order/payment/refund:** order and payment are separate state machines; `draft → submitted → accepted → fulfilling → fulfilled` with cancel/backorder/return paths; payment transitions only from verified provider events; refund is a new signed ledger event, not a deletion.
- **Inventory:** `available → reserved → committed/issued` or `released`; receipt/adjustment/transfer/consumption are immutable movements with source, quantity, unit, lot and actor. Concurrent reservation must be atomic and reject negative stock unless the workspace policy explicitly permits it.
- **Subscription/entitlement:** `trial → active → grace → suspended/cancelled/expired`; invoice/payment and entitlement changes are independently recorded; retries are idempotent and a downgrade is effective at the stated time.
- **Automation run:** `queued → running → waiting/awaiting_approval → succeeded/failed/cancelled`; retain input/output references, step attempts, actor and correlation ID; retry/dead-letter/replay must not duplicate external effects.

Every transition needs: allowed source states, required permission, field/data validation, transaction boundary, idempotency key, event emitted, notification policy, retry/compensation behavior, audit record and user-visible recovery. Invalid transitions return a useful conflict with current state; the UI must not optimistically show a saved result before durable persistence.

## AI action and function catalog

The assistant is a permissioned operator over the same application functions users see. It must call typed server actions, never bypass business logic by writing tables directly, inventing success, or treating a visible control as authorization.

| Function class | Representative tools | Preconditions and confirmation |
|---|---|---|
| Read and explain | `search_contacts`, `get_customer_timeline`, `get_booking_availability`, `get_order_status`, `get_ticket`, `get_stock_summary`, `get_plan_usage`, `get_report`. | Validate workspace/branch and object-level read permission; return source time and state; redact sensitive fields based on caller role; distinguish no result from provider failure. |
| Prepare a change | `draft_reply`, `draft_quote`, `propose_booking`, `prepare_order_change`, `prepare_refund`, `prepare_workflow_change`, `summarize_case`. | Return a structured preview with target, values, terms, source facts and warnings; do not create an external side effect during planning. |
| Low-risk internal writes | `add_note`, `create_task`, `assign_queue`, `tag_record`, `create_draft`, `record_call_summary`. | Require write permission, idempotency key, validation, audit actor and durable response; show what persisted. |
| Customer-visible actions | `send_message`, `confirm_booking`, `send_quote`, `submit_order`, `cancel_service`, `publish_content`. | Check channel consent, recipient and current state; preview consequential content/terms; require explicit confirmation per tenant policy; verify provider acceptance and delivery state separately. |
| Financial, regulated or destructive actions | `capture_payment`, `issue_refund`, `change_subscription`, `delete_or_export_data`, `approve_claim`, `change_medical_record`, `release_quality_hold`, `change_access`. | Enforce narrow role, step-up/second approval when configured, immutable audit, policy validation and post-action reconciliation; AI may prepare but must not self-approve. High-impact decisions remain with designated humans. |

Every tool has a versioned schema with `action_id`, workspace/object identifiers, expected version, arguments, preconditions, permission, confirmation level, idempotency key, result state, audit reference and recoverable error. The assistant explains the pending action in plain language, rechecks state immediately before commit, and reports the persisted result and any partial failure. Repeated or concurrent requests return the same safe result rather than duplicate charges, bookings, messages or orders.

## Integration functions and lifecycle

Each connector is a per-workspace connection object, not a provider-wide secret shared in UI code. It must support: start consent/OAuth or documented setup; validate scopes and account identity; encrypt credentials at rest; list enabled capabilities; sync cursors and webhooks; verify signatures; deduplicate deliveries; enforce provider and tenant rate limits; map errors to retryable/permanent; refresh credentials; handle subscription expiry; test connection; disconnect/revoke; export/delete according to retention; rotate secrets; show audit and health. Make unsupported actions visibly unavailable.

The same lifecycle applies to email (Gmail/Microsoft 365), officially supported WhatsApp Business onboarding, eligible Meta pages/inboxes, YouTube-supported comments/moderation, SMS/telephony, ecommerce/ERP/accounting, calendars, storage and websites. Per provider, document exact scopes, account eligibility, supported inbound/outbound operations, quota/pricing, webhook validation, retention and reconnect. QR login is considered only if the provider officially supports the selected business-account flow; do not collect consumer credentials or imply arbitrary QR sessions are approved.

Website integration must offer an accessible hosted form/widget, signed short-lived session tokens, domain verification/allowlist, CORS/CSP guidance, spam/rate controls, consent, data mapping, submission receipts, error recovery, mobile behavior, theme configuration and a customer-owned-site acceptance test. Public submissions stay isolated from admin APIs and cannot choose arbitrary tenant or agent IDs.

## Product types and configuration model

At onboarding, ask a short set of questions: business model(s), locations/branches, customer vocabulary, primary journey, catalog/services, booking or fulfillment needs, team roles, connected channels, billing currency/time zone, sensitive-data needs and desired automation. Generate a reviewable draft configuration; the customer confirms it before publishing. The tenant admin can configure modules and quotas only inside owner-issued entitlements; the owner sets the maximum entitlements, license, model/provider availability and support policy.

Represent type-specific behavior in versioned metadata/configuration where practical (field definitions, statuses, forms, policies, templates and dashboards). Use domain-specific services for invariants such as capacity, payments, inventory, consent and healthcare privacy; do not attempt to encode those safety rules as arbitrary prompt text. Include migration/versioning for template changes and preserve a workspace’s customized version.

Profile acceptance should be measurable: setup completes without an engineer; one core journey succeeds with synthetic and real approved integration events; save/reload preserves fields and terms; an unauthorized role is blocked; duplicate webhook/action is idempotent; provider/database failure has a useful recovery; exports and audit reconcile; module removal does not corrupt shared records; and accessibility checks pass.

## Delivery sequence and acceptance

0. **Protect and finish current work:** preserve both checkouts and server changes; complete client image verification, clean installation, license/integrity tests, billing and branding acceptance. The merge stays stopped per user instruction. Voice browser and telephone acceptance remain owned by the other workstream.
1. **Discovery and architecture:** inventory existing routes, schemas and UI; map customer journeys and gaps; define module contracts, tenant data ownership, roles, provider capability matrix, resource budgets, migration and deployment design. Select first target industry using customer evidence; do not assume a market priority is already approved.
2. **Foundation:** deliver tenant-safe contacts, conversations, roles, audit, event ingestion and resilient automation execution. Verify duplicate events, concurrent updates, cross-tenant access, backups and upgrade paths.
3. **CRM and website journey:** connect a third-party site form/widget to lead capture, assignment, staff reply and timeline. Test anonymous abuse, delivery failures, consent and mobile accessibility.
4. **First channel and shared inbox:** implement an officially supported business messaging connection and email integration; prove onboarding, permissions, inbound/outbound, disconnect, renewal, opt-out and recovery with test accounts and provider-approved flows.
5. **Automation and AI actions:** implement configurable triggers/actions and human handoff, then prove an end-to-end inquiry-to-appointment journey without duplicates. Confirm consequential actions; verify voice and text use the same tenant-scoped business state.
6. **First vertical:** ship one complete, selected business workflow with real users, operational reporting, import/export, billing and documented support. Only add medical or regulated capabilities after applicable review.
7. **Additional verticals and channels:** expand from measured demand using the catalog above, with provider-specific acceptance and independent module entitlements. Avoid claiming broad channel support from one successful adapter.
8. **Commercial acceptance:** verify owner/client edition separation, owner-only entitlements, licensing and revocation, plan downgrade, audit, recovery, observability, load and capacity on representative 4-core/8-16 GB hosts as well as larger deployments. Test fresh import and rollback, localized invoice/tax cases, support escalation, AI quality/cost gates and the client tamper threat model. Publish tested requirements rather than assumed hardware guarantees.
9. **Integration and release:** only after the user lifts the merge hold, obtain a stable core voice checkpoint, integrate in a separate checkout preserving both sides, resolve conflicts, run migrations and targeted/full checks, stage and test the complete combined revision, and document rollback before any production release.
